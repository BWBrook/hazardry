P7 completed a solo Iron & Ruin session under revision 4. Sera rescued Nima and the tithe ledger, returned for the remaining captives, broke the sluice grille with a Wrack, and stopped the branding mechanism before escaping. It was coherent, active play with consequential resource choices. These are orchestrator observations, not Fable's pending independent audit and not balance evidence.

Fortune reached zero in the first act, but its milestone refill and favourable later rolls meant it was not scarce in act two. Sera paid blood for her Weave, used Heroic Act, chose a Wrack, then used a physical Brawn test for the final machinery. The last test rolled a natural one. No seed was chosen or retried to obtain an outcome.

The threat advanced on the gate order, alarm, reserve dispatch, threatened travel and furnace start. Success did not freeze the opposition. A failed attempt to knock the brazier away left it threatening the cage; Nima's independent success stopped a message, but did not stop Varkos acting on his own. This matches revision 4's intended procedure. The changed skin, scenario, party size and player prevent attributing differences from P6 to the guidance alone.

## Pacing and presentation

The eight beats are fewer than Almanac 9's illustrative ten, but both acts reached genuine turns and an ending; no quota was imposed. There was no stall requiring the protocol's neutral prompt. The adventure offered concrete objectives and a plausible reason for Sera to take risks. Nima, Ossian and Tovin had distinct wants and voices, though Varkos largely appeared through orders.

There were repeated commitment pauses. G002, G005, G008, G012, G017, G019, G021 and G023 state risks after the player's initial intent and ask for commitment. This usually preserves informed choice correctly because the earlier open question did not contain those stakes, but it lengthens the session. Earlier concrete options with full stakes could avoid several exchanges under the existing brief. G009 reopens the Weave payment after Sera had already chosen blood; this preserves a legal post-roll choice but adds another short exchange. Do not remove meaningful discretionary decisions simply to reduce reply count.

The opposition and rising water created movement, but danger often became another opportunity to intervene rather than an immediate loss. That is a play-quality question for the audit, not proof of an erroneous rule. The all-perilous beat labels also deserve scrutiny, especially the brief hatch transition (beat 4) and final escape (beat 8), both resolved without a roll. The handbook allows consequential hazards resolved narratively to qualify; successful dice are not the criterion.

The fresh CLI player was a setup workaround for occupied collaboration slots, not a repeated or substituted player. Access remains instructed rather than enforced; the full player native trace shows no tools. The Custodian's command record is not a full access trace. No crisis table or hidden scenario text was intentionally relayed, and no such disclosure was observed in public play; the independent auditor should assess leakage.

All records are frozen for Fable. Next-cycle discussion should focus on reducing avoidable commitment pauses, maintaining a current recap at closure, and clarifying any disputed spell/attack or combat-boundary rulings. P7 does not warrant a rule change by itself. No source, engine or guidance edits were made during play, and no findings were repaired retroactively.
