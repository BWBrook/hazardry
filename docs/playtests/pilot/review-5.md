## Observed under revision 6

**Actor-selection safeguards held; ability-choice handling remained incomplete.**
- No Custodian chose a roller silently, by odds or by majority.
- Every meaningful, affordable die nudge was offered at its exact price:
  - P9: 3, 3, 3 and 1 beads;
  - P12: 4 tokens.
  - Players took some and declined others.
- The exception is ability choices. P9's Insight was missed several times, and at G008 an affordable nudge-plus-Insight option was withheld (item 2 below). The P9 post-audit note withdraws any blanket claim that every option was offered.
- Unaffordable spends were explained.
- The one retry, P9's second questioning of Lambert, was justified by new leverage.
- P12's two real disagreements were each returned to the players and resolved by their own choices:
  - Orthe's terms (G007–G009);
  - the caveat (G013–G015).

**Records improved.**
- Both runs kept every checkpoint version and delivered every public body, including the closing one.
- Both kept each player's incoming messages.
- P12 confirmed all 43 inputs by independent reconstruction (not a replay of play).

**What still went wrong:**
1. **Actor collisions cost round trips (P9).**
   - With both players answering at once, they repeatedly claimed the same roll. Once, each then deferred to the other.
   - Six of 27 replies were pure pauses. The Custodian ended one deadlock with an opt-in tie-break die (both players had to agree).
   - The players learned to settle collisions within their own declarations by mid-act 2. P12's three players rarely bid for the same roll.
2. **Ability offers missed or mis-sequenced:**
   - P9's Insight was withheld or offered late several times, and once wrongly called spent (G008), which withheld an affordable option.
   - P12 omitted a squarely fitting tag's Advantage (G011).
   - Revision 6's correct-numbers rule covers these cases, but the brief does not say so specifically.
3. **Private material in narration (P9).**
   - G016 published Wulfric's private boundary as a guarantee, and private clocks were named in public. Under the Leaks clause, P9 counts as a harness test only.
   - The disclosure was small, but the rule is clear.
4. **Scenario fidelity (P9):**
   - a clock tick missed on an independent trigger (G006);
   - a timetable changed after the freeze (Pell's departure);
   - a discovery cap miscounted;
   - a seed reused.
5. **Perilous flags are judgement calls, applied generously at times.**
   - P12 beat 5 contains the asking for, negotiating, accepting and implementing of Orthe's hold. G008 says the hold has not begun and that another unprotected cycle brings a crisis. The players earned safety within the beat, and overcoming a peril does not erase it, so the flag stands as a judgement call.
   - P12 beat 8 is the weaker flag: a safe shed with mild stakes, though documentary failure before the courier returned could have cost the immediate goal.
   - P9's final hearing was flagged perilous though its chosen options were unrolled and guaranteed (Astra's audit: a judgement call).
   - Earlier generous flags were noted in P7 and P8. This is a qualified cross-run observation, not a count of established errors by either team.
   - **Sensitivity, under the same third-perilous-beat policy:** removing either P12 beat 5 or beat 8 moves the second milestone to beat 10. Removing both leaves flags at beats 1, 3, 6, 7 and 10: the first milestone at beat 6 and only one due by close. The recorded caveat die (4) succeeds against INT 15 or INT 16 alike. This is a sensitivity check, not a reconstructed alternate run.
6. **My relay omissions (P9).** Two of Anselm's answers never reached Hawise verbatim.

## Pressure, and Barry's direction on skin fuses

- **P9 does not show** that honest play switches Briar's Sin off. The scenario's narrow ambient policy, the Abbot's licence and the missed Chapter tick all restricted exposure.
- **P12 shows a thematic tension.** Additive core triggers brought Time Odyssey's temporal-paradox fuse to Anomaly 4 from generic threat (a flood). The run's historical interventions succeeded, and so avoided their stated failure charges. This is not noncompliance with the frozen rules; the fuse simply filled from something other than its theme.

**Barry's decision**, recorded below, is that skin fuses may work distinctively:
- **Briar & Benedictine.** Sin is doctrinal, not only vice. Characters can act in good conscience or out of necessity and still sin by doctrine.
  - **At setup:** the vows and doctrine are set out publicly.
  - **During play:** the Custodian does not volunteer the Sin price of a deed, but answers honestly when asked what the character would know. It applies Sin after the deed and explains why. Roll stakes are still stated before the dice.
  - **Ambient exposure** comes from events (proximity to evil, desecration, sacred horror), never from a quota.
  - **A superior's permission** does not automatically absolve.
- **In general.** Each skin may say which core triggers feed its distinctive fuse, and in what fictional form, so that generic danger feeds a themed fuse only where the skin says it does.

Both are Stage 3 rules items: publication and adoption wait for Stage 3. Before then, the Briar treatment gets a separately labelled probe, and the general mapping is tested as an experimental pilot variant (proposal C).

## Proposals: protocol revision 7, for P10, P13 and P11

**A. Player brief.** Add one line: "When a companion may want the same roll or role, say in your answer who should take it, or say that you accept a tie-break."

**B. Custodian brief:**
- "When players claim the same single roll, ask them to choose, and offer an opt-in tie-break die (a harness draw, used only if all claimants agree)."
- "Before rolling, check relevant tags and modifiers and offer optional powers at their costs; after resolution, offer newly eligible abilities. Preserve the player's choice, and settle all offered once-per-scene abilities before closing the scene." (Astra's wording: automatic modifiers apply; optional powers are offered, never bought for the player.)
- "Narrate what characters observe. Do not publish an NPC's private boundaries or a private clock's name or value as fact; show signs."
- "Keep the frozen scenario's triggers and timetable. A trigger that does not depend on failure fires on success too."
- "Flag a beat perilous when failure at its consequential choice could cost Stamina, a life or the goal. A safeguard earned within the beat does not erase the peril already overcome; routine work that begins after safety is established is not perilous merely because a goal remains pending." (Astra's wording.)

**C. Scenario setup: an experimental pilot variant.** Selecting which core triggers feed a skin's fuse changes the present additive default (this protocol's Custodian brief and Almanac 4), so it is labelled as an experiment, not adopted baseline. For P10 and P13:
- the scenario declares, before play, which core triggers feed the skin's fuse, in what fictional form, and any exclusions; the mapping is frozen with the scenario;
- player-relevant changes go in the player packets;
- the manifest and the audit record the override.

Barry approved the direction ("both Briar-specific and the more general skin-distinctive triggers") and asked us to action this review. Adoption remains a Stage 3 decision.

**D. Orchestrator.**
- Build each relay message from the record, not by hand, so that no completed answer is missed. Fable composes relays with a per-player cursor script; Astra's relay already does this.
- Before launching roles, inspect the selected skin and public boundaries and each character's own content in the packets, then verify the actual sent bytes by hash (P12's attempt A).
- Compare each exact returned public text to its checkpoint version; keep any mismatch or intervention, as with P12's G015.
- Check the draw index for every new dice command, tie-breaks included, and keep the index and event ID on retries (P9's reused seed). This prevents errors without resampling.
- P12's 43-input check is an independent reconstruction, not a replay of play.
