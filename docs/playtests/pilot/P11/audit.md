**Wrong — G014 used a diagnostic command as live play and omitted the granted Advantage.** The raw event `p11-redd-bargain` (sequence 31) has `method: "x"`, `stakes: "x"` and no Advantage. The failed attempt to replace it with different text was correctly rejected by event-id protection. **Wrong — the two-Luck rebate was an invented rule.** Under the actual dice, four tokens are needed to win: moving either die by three only ties margins, and ties go to the defender (Manual 1.4). Nothing in the printed Luck rules authorizes a conditional two-token reimbursement for a Custodian error. P014 initially accepts the rebate while changing the offer; after G015 separates the choices, P015 declines the original deal. No rebate or bargain nudge was ultimately applied. Thus the resource ledger is unaffected by the proposed rebate, but “Dell did not take it” needs that qualification.

**Correct in part, judgement call in part — the fresh favour offer (G015–G016).** Refusing to use the doses roll to win a different bargain was correct. A brokered favour is a genuinely new approach and stated cost, satisfying Manual 5.1 and Almanac 2; this was not simply the same offer rolled again. However, the original failure was advertised as arbitration, one hour and the loss of a sound case (G013–G015), not just rejection of the doses. The new offer bypassed those promised consequences. A reasonable reading is that a new settlement can avert arbitration before it starts; the public wording did not state that contingency. I therefore accept eligibility for a fresh roll but do not endorse an unqualified claim that all original failure stakes were honoured. Future stakes should distinguish rejection of an offer from an already-triggered arbitration or transfer; the record stays as played.

What is established is **zero recorded gain despite a matching written trigger**, not the counterfactual final track. The ordinary zero-cost choices elsewhere were legitimate: shelter avoided dust, the crew left before the open-Glass time charge, no natural 20 occurred, and the bounded broker's favour carried no declared dangerous obligation satisfying the local desperate-bargain policy. No Luck was spent to avoid Pressure.

**Descriptively correct, evidentially overstated — five of six zero-gain records.** The summaries for P8, P9, P10, P12, P13 and P11 record gains of 0, 0, 0, 4, 0 and 0. P9 was already harness-only; P11 now has a leak and a missed trigger. The runs also differ in rules variants, scenes, party choices and exposures. That count is useful as an observation about these implementations, not six clean tests demonstrating that correctly applied Pressure rarely moves. The next review should separate absent opportunities, successfully avoided triggers and missed charges. It should not infer a quota or a new fuse rate from this aggregate.

## Scenes, peril and closure

**Correct — beats 1 and 2 are perilous; seven beats can be a genuine ending.** The live load/hot alcove and collapsing arch carried real danger; preparation and success do not remove that danger retrospectively. The ending resolves the haul, ownership, treatment and dusk deadline. Almanac 9 explicitly calls its pacing numbers typical, not quotas (lines 188–204). Three beats in act one and seven overall are short, but padding them or staging danger would be worse adherence to the rule. The shortfall is descriptive, not itself a failure.

**Judgement call, defensible — beat 3 is non-perilous.** The chosen honest declaration risked one hour and temporary custody pending Hale's ruling. The stated stakes did not establish permanent loss of the haul, an imminent missed deadline or physical harm. Dell's belief that it decided the whole run does not expand the Custodian's declared failure. The unchosen lock-breaking route cannot by itself make the chosen action perilous.

**Wrong — omitting peril from Pell's rescue (beat 5).** Pell had only until dusk, Sula lacked sufficient stock, and the crew surrendered sale value to provide the needed treatment. His survival was still explicitly uncertain. A life was at stake in this scene, and the safeguard was earned inside it. That meets Almanac 9's life limb without a PC test. “Talk with no stakes beyond mood” and routine completion after established safety do not describe this rescue. The report's claim that beats 3–7 risked no life overlooks Pell.

**Judgement call, favouring peril — beat 6.** The ownership dispute concerned the central haul and Dell's debt goal, under a closing weigh-in. Losing a sound case through arbitration would materially reduce the small haul and its ability to pay the debt; there is a strong goal-based case for peril. A narrow definition of the episode's goal as bringing *any* useful haul home supports the opposite call. That scope should be explicit rather than decided from the absence of Stamina damage.

This leaves **three well-supported perilous scenes, or four if the Scale House dispute qualifies**. With the latter reading, one milestone was due by beat 6; with only three, the allowed third-or-fourth cadence leaves its timing discretionary. The categorical explanation “only two, so none due” is wrong. I do not assert an unconditional missed award independently of that remaining goal-scope judgment, and no advancement is backfilled. The log also closes beat 1 after the arch roll, assigning the first two draws to scene 1 (sequences 9–16). That ordering did not refresh any used power here, but scene-dependent analysis must follow the fiction rather than infer boundaries solely from roll metadata.

**Wrong — G008 narrows a public option during the role collision.** G007 allowed all three to visit Pell while Hale held the cases. G008 says only two can go, requiring someone to remain, without a changed circumstance. All players had selected splitting up, so the two clarification replies were understandable and the opt-in die correctly stayed unused. Still, the full-party option should have remained available. Revision 8 already covers collisions over one “roll or role”; this is an application problem, not evidence that the rule only addresses dice. A first-named-volunteer default would be a new priority rule and is not established as necessary by this example.

## Modules, state and the proposed fixes

**Correct at the observed steps — Radiation and Wealth.** Rue's starting dose is recorded, and her tier-1 wash removes it once (event 26). Shielding, shelter and leaving Case Five avert further exposure as declared. Wealth 1 covers the tier-1 duty and wash without loss; two sealed cases clear Debt 3→1, and the agreed share payment lowers Wealth 1→0 (events 37–38). The sale arithmetic and opened-case cost were stated before commitment. Gifts change ownership without producing Wealth. Upper condition steps, Attention/Heat, below-tier Luck purchases, crisis, combat and acute treatment remain untested. The custom Radiation details are campaign policies under the optional module, not newly printed core rules.

**Wrong, minor — carried inventory was not reconciled.** Rue's final sheet still lists the 40-foot rope and two flares, although the rope was left in the cellar and G005 consumed one flare. The session memory remembers the rope location, but the sheet can mislead a later public export. Preserve this final snapshot; any continuation should reconcile inventory explicitly rather than silently replenish it.

**Core change — one reproduced P2 integration gap.** The new play-only entry, prompts, ordinary actions and published-release exclusions pass the checks in [core-harness-review.md](core-harness-review.md). At Pressure 5, however, the engine requires a nonempty d6 `--table-result`, despite the core having no mandatory crisis table. An honest tableless consequence is rejected and the next ordinary action stays blocked. P11 supplied a custom table and never reached a crisis, so it did not expose this gap. It is an existing engine assumption newly reached by the supported core entry, not evidence that the new default attributes or release filters are wrong.

**Recommendations, not implemented rules changes.** NPC syntax is already stated in `opposed --help`: `Sheet stem or npc:NAME`. Better unresolved-name diagnostics may help; accepting ambiguous bare names is not required. Existing `--dry-run` prevents persistence but still samples and displays dice, so it is not a no-draw validation mode. A true validation-only path is the clearer diagnostic proposal. If mistaken pending rolls can later be voided, preserve the original event, dice, reason and explicit replacement provenance; do not erase history or create a discretionary silent reroll. Likewise, error compensation needs an explicit agreed policy before use, not an improvised Luck credit selected after seeing the result.

The findings are sufficient for review 7. They do not authorize source fixes, a replacement pilot or retrospective repairs. Keep the story and raw mechanics intact, classify this record as harness-only, and carry the reproduced core-crisis gap and the unresolved error-recovery policy into the next bounded work plan.
