# P11 report (orchestrator)

P11 was the last ordinary pilot run: the Hazardry core with no skin, three salvagers, two optional modules on, under protocol revision 8.
- **The setting.** Ferrous Gap, a scrap town at the edge of the Glass, a contaminated zone. The run used a play-only `core` harness entry, added for the purpose (`a2ce6f1`).
- **The modules.** A per-character Radiation track (Condition Tracks) and a party Wealth score (Wealth & Attention).

**The episode,** *The Blue Stack*, was a tight salvage-and-reckoning story:
- a hot cellar, a fraying line and a rival crew racing a dust front;
- an underground route out, ending at a Wardens' grate;
- a dying man who sold the same share twice;
- a cure the town cannot make for itself.

**The players' choices.** They chose honesty and mercy at every turn, and the scenario priced those choices fairly:
- they declared their load at the grate;
- they opened a case of antidote for the dying man;
- they refused the lender's offer to clear their debt by striking the girl's share;
- they paid her share from their own Wealth;
- they gave the remaining doses to the town clinic by deed.

The crew ended with Debt 1 and Wealth 0. The dying man lives.

**Shape:** 7 beats in two acts (3 + 4), 2 perilous, 19 Custodian replies, 5 player-character rolls, and Pressure 0. All checkpoints matched, all 45 player deliveries verified, and no player used tools beyond their packet. These are the orchestrator's notes; Astra's audit decides the rules questions.

## What happened

**Act 1: the Glass (beats 1–3).**
- **The Spindle Yard** (beat 1, perilous).
  - The cold open: Rue is in the hot alcove, the winch line is fraying, flares are on the ridge and a dust wall is closing in.
  - The crew rigged Rue's rope rather than risk the double-block roll. They hauled three of six cases in an hour and a half, leaving before the hour that would have charged Pressure for time under threat.
  - Hollis's free counter sweep found Case Five warm. He shielded it (INT with Advantage, success), and the crew still left it behind.
- **The Drain** (beat 2, perilous).
  - They chose the underground route, out of the riders' sight and sheltered from the dust.
  - Hollis shored the sagging third arch rather than rush it (success). The dust front passed overhead while they were below: real shelter, so no dose.
- **The Sluice grate** (beat 3, act end).
  - Prying the lock would have meant smuggling, and Pell's map warned against it. Dell declared the three cases to the patrol instead (EMP; Advantage for Hollis's honest readings; a miss, turned into a success with 4 Luck).
  - The Custodian corrected Dell's invented "Scale House stamp" to the true pre-Glass pharmacy stamp, and kept the successful roll.

**Act 2: the Gap (beats 4–7).**
- **The Gate house** (beat 4).
  - Wren brings word that her father Pell is dying and is asking for the crew.
  - Who would stay to list the cases became a three-way, then a two-way, role collision: two pure pauses, settled when Rue gave way. Dell listed the cases; Rue and Hollis went to the Wash.
- **The Wash** (beat 5).
  - Rue washed off her dose (tier 1). Sula identified the cases as Blue Salt and said Pell needs most of a case.
  - Dell chose to open one: "I don't trade a man's last day for a ledger line."
  - At Dell's side, Pell confessed that he had sold his 40% twice. The rival boss, Redd, held the earlier signed paper.
- **The Scale House** (beat 6).
  - The Custodian's command slip drew the first bargain roll without the agreed Advantage (below). Dell lost it and would not spend Luck on a deal that gave the clinic's last doses to Redd.
  - On a fresh approach, a broker's favour owed, Dell won. Redd tore up his paper.
  - Dell refused Vane's offer to clear the whole debt by striking Wren's share. He sold the two sealed cases at weight (Debt 3 → 1). The crew paid Wren from Wealth (1 → 0) at Rue's and Hollis's insistence: a worthless warm case is not a share.
- **The dusk bell** (beat 7, act end). The opened case was deeded to the Wash; nothing was forfeit at the bell; Pell will see the morning. Closing question: thirty names are waiting on eight doses, and two sealed cases are still in a cellar the rival crew now knows about.

## Revision 8 against its aims

**Scenes and peril.**
- Beats 1 and 2 were flagged perilous, and the shoring is a real trap defeated by preparation (revision 8's own example).
- Beats 3–7 were not flagged. Their failures cost time, goods or standing, not Stamina, a life or the goal. Where the goal was at stake (beat 6's bargain), failure meant arbitration rather than loss of the goal.
- **Pacing.** Act 1 had only three beats and the session seven, short of "about ten". The Custodian said plainly that it would not stage danger to reach a milestone, so no milestone came.
- **Audit question:** were beats 3 or 6 perilous by the "goal" limb?

**Help.** The rule was used twice and works.
- At the grate, Dell asked, and the Custodian gave Advantage for Hollis's readings, with its reason and "it does not stack" stated before the dice.
- At the Scale House, Dell asked again, and Advantage was granted for Vane's own rule and Case Five on her bridge. The command slip then dropped it.

**Collisions.**
- Two pure pauses (G008, G009) came from one role question: who stays to list the cases. Each player first nominated himself or herself to go, then two each nominated themselves to stay. This is the same oscillation as P9's G012–G013.
- The Custodian asked each time, offered a three-way and then a two-way tie-break, and refused to count Hollis's vote.
- **Lesson.** The revision 7 player line ("say in your answer who should take it, or accept a tie-break") does not stop self-nomination when the claim is about where to go, not which roll to make.
- **Note.** G008 said "up to two of you can go", whereas G007's option 2 had let all three go to Pell with the cases held at the Gate. That narrowed an option already offered.

**Hazards and frozen solutions.** The Custodian kept the frozen consequences and gave fair warning:
- Pell's map note telegraphed Sula's refusal to treat smugglers ("Sula turned the Kemp boys out of the Wash for this. Don't.").
- It answered "is there another way out?" honestly from the frozen text.
- It refused Dell's request to rewrite the bargain after the dice: "the stakes were fixed before the dice". This is the correct ruling.

**Private material.**
- Clocks were never named.
- **Audit question:** some "Where this stands" summaries stated NPC lines as fact:
  - "Vane honours the earliest signed paper";
  - "Wren will take nothing that is not owed".

  Vane's line came first from Pell's testimony in the fiction. Teague's ("clean title only") had been put in the player-facing summary before play.

**Player-character agency.**
- The Custodian treated Dell's instructions to Rue and Hollis as suggestions, and asked Rue and Hollis directly whether they would follow him.
- It never wrote dialogue for a player character.

**Records.**
- The orchestrator alone wrote the transcript.
- The closing followed turn-loop step 6 exactly: closing lines, then one archive, then receipts.
- The Custodian twice re-saved a checkpoint after a wording fix and logged each fix publicly.

## The Custodian's harness error (G014)

**What happened.**
- While working out why `play.py opposed --opponent redd` failed, the Custodian ran a persisting `opposed --defer` with placeholder text and without the agreed Advantage, where it should have used `--dry-run`. That drew seed 111004 as a real roll.
- A retry with the correct text was refused, because the event ID was already used for a different command. No void command exists.

**What the Custodian did.**
- Disclosed the slip publicly before Dell's choice.
- Kept the dice as drawn and added a System note to the public log.
- **Invented a remedy:** spend 4 Luck to win, get 2 back. This is not a printed rule.

**Outcome.** Dell declined to spend, so the remedy never took effect, and his next approach was rolled correctly (111005).

**What I did.** The protocol lets the orchestrator correct a malformed command but never a die, and correcting this one would have needed a second die, so I did not intervene.

**For the audit and Stage 4:**
- whether the remedy was legitimate;
- a way to void a mistaken pending roll;
- making diagnostic runs dry by default.

## Modules exercised

**Radiation (Condition Track).**
- **Exercised:**
  - one exposure, Rue's dose at the start;
  - the declared sources (the alcove, Case Five, hot veins, the Bellmouth, the dust fill), each telegraphed;
  - the shielding procedure;
  - removal by wash (1 → 0).
- **Avoided by player choice:** the Drain kept them out of the dust and the veins, Case Five was left behind, and the arch was shored.
- **Not reached:** steps 2–5, so neither collapse nor treatment was ever tested.

**Wealth & Attention.**
- **Exercised:**
  - tiers (tier 1 duty and a wash paid without lowering Wealth);
  - sale pricing (Vane at weight clearing Debt; Teague's pair rounding, stated and weighed);
  - a share paid from Wealth (1 → 0).
- **Not reached:** Wealth never rose to 3, so Attention and Heat never arose, and no Wealth Luck test was called.
- The Custodian disclosed the economics honestly, including that two sealed cases were worth the same as three at Teague's price, which made opening one cheaper.

**Pressure.** 0 throughout. That makes five of the last six runs with no Pressure (P8, P9, P10, P13 and P11; the exception was P12's four, from time under threat), this time under the additive default. Every Pressure-bearing option was priced and avoided:
- staying for the second hour;
- smuggling through the grate;
- an unrolled natural 20;
- a bargain made under threat.

## Coherence and fun

- **The setting and NPCs.** The Gap's economy, its Wardens and its lender were coherent, and the NPCs had real lines, shown in play.
- **Temperaments carried the moral weight.** Dell's client line held against his debt drive three times: the confession, the clause, and Wren's share. Hollis's honest readings were used as evidence twice. Rue's "never leave anyone" sent her to Pell's cot.
- **The players reasoned like a crew.** Hollis's message on the doses changed Dell's offer.
- **An Innes-like shape,** as Barry noted: hard work in a hostile landscape, then a reckoning with ledgers, papers and conscience.

## What to change before the programme

1. **Role collisions about where to go,** not only which roll to make. The tie-break and the player line reduce pauses but do not stop self-nomination in both directions. One more player-brief clause might help: "if you and a companion both volunteer, defer to the first named unless you have a reason."
2. **Harness.**
   - A way to void a mistaken pending roll.
   - `play.py opposed --opponent NAME` should accept the NPC's name, or say clearly how to name an NPC.
   - Diagnostic runs should be dry by default.
3. **Custodian remedies for its own errors.** Should a Custodian ever compensate a player for its own error with resources? The handbook is silent.
4. **Conditions and Wealth coverage.** Neither module reached its upper steps. A labelled probe would be needed to test Radiation collapse and treatment, Attention and Heat, and a below-tier Wealth Luck test.
5. **Pressure.** Five of the last six runs had no Pressure, across skins, mappings and the base game. This is the strongest single pilot result. It belongs in the post-pilot comparison with Astra, not in a quota.

## Post-audit note

Added by Fable on 4 October 2026, after Astra's audit (`audit.md`, with `core-harness-review.md`; board 931). I checked its main findings against the raw records and accept the audit in full. It corrects or qualifies this report as follows:

- **P11 counts as a harness test only.** G012's summary published Wren's private line ("will take nothing that is not owed"; frozen scenario line 51) while she stood silent, and nothing public had established it. G017 then said she had already said it, and P017 Rue relied on it. Revision 8 makes any run where private material reaches players a harness test. Vane's paper rule is different: Pell had just described it aloud, so only the narrator's categorical wording was wrong.
- **G002 also leaked**, beyond the clock's name: it forecast the hidden riders' timetable, not just the cost of staying. G003 corrected the charge's scope but did not undo the forecast.
- **A Pressure charge was missed.** Rue held a flare to light the arch (P004, G005), and frozen policy line 15 charges +1 for a flare in the Glass or the Gap. No charge, warning or exemption was declared. The rival clock rightly did not tick underground, since it needs the *open* Glass, but that does not waive the Pressure line. My statement that "every Pressure-bearing option was priced and avoided" is wrong. The ending is not reset to 1: a proper warning could have changed the choice.
- **"Five of the last six runs at zero"** is true as a count but is not six clean demonstrations: P9 and P11 are harness tests, and P11 has a missed charge. Review 7 separates absent, avoided and missed triggers.
- **Beat 5 was perilous.** Pell's life was at stake until dusk, and the crew gave up sale value to save him. My "no life at stake in beats 3–7" overlooked an NPC's life.
- **Beat 6 is a judgement call**, which Astra leaves open. My own reading: losing a sound case to arbitration would have materially threatened the debt goal, so it was perilous by revision 8's goal example. That gives four perilous beats and a milestone due by beat 6. With three, its timing was discretionary. Either way, "only two, so none due" was wrong. Nothing is backfilled.
- **Beat 3 non-perilous** stands as defensible.
- **G014.** The diagnostic roll and the dropped Advantage were real errors, and the Luck rebate was an invented rule. Moving either die by 3 only tied the margins, and ties go to Redd, so the spend needed was 4. P014 at first accepted the rebate while changing the offer; P015 then declined the original deal. No rebate or spend was ever applied. The fresh favour offer was a new approach and could be rolled, but the original failure stakes (arbitration, an hour and a sound case) sounded automatic and were bypassed without a stated contingency.
- **G008 narrowed an offered option** without any change in the fiction. Revision 8's "roll or role" line already covers this; it was an application error. I withdraw my "defer to the first named" proposal, which would add a priority rule that this case does not show is needed.
- **Inventory.** Rue's final sheet still lists the rope left in the cellar and two flares, though one burned out. The snapshot is preserved; a continuation would reconcile it explicitly.
- **Harness.** The `npc:NAME` syntax is already in `opposed --help`, so better errors may suffice. `--dry-run` still samples and shows dice, so it is not a validation-only mode. Any void must keep the original draw and record the replacement.
- **Core harness (a2ce6f1).** Ordinary core play works. A tableless crisis cannot be recorded: `play.py` requires `--table-result` with d6 faces, and core prints no table. That assumption is older than the commit; the commit exposed it. P11 never reached a crisis.
