# P10 independent audit

P10 is a complete two-act session under protocol revision 7. I compared the exact public transcript with the frozen scenario and policies, the as-closed campaign log and receipts, and the Service Duct Blues and core rules. The verdict below is for this run only; the fuse mapping is an experimental variant, not an adopted rules change. The 10 recorded beats and 14 PC rolls are real. Most procedures were handled cleanly. The consequential error is the non-perilous flag on the time-sensitive act-one repair; several lesser points concern explicit help rulings and preserving player agency.

## Mechanical audit

**Correct — the 14 checks and their settlement.** The log's `raw_roll` events use seeds 110001–110014, in transcript order. I checked each target, dice, kept die, margin, Advantage source and narration against G002, G005–G006, G008, G010, G012, G014–G015, G017–G018 and G020. The four Quick Scans used SYS with Advantage, each on a new situation and within its scene's free use (`skins/service_duct_blues.md:76–82`). Sefa's two kept natural 1s (G005, G020) were locked and each yielded one extra sensor finding. Imre's two “Reroute!” uses were in separate scenes and each cost 1 RES; Jun's Ops/Security Override gave Advantage for 1 RES on the cutoff. The three failed social dice had exact, affordable nudge offers: 5 chits at G006, 3 at G014, 5 at G018. Imre declined, paid, and declined respectively. He was not rerolled, and only his paid nudge changed an outcome. The two actor collisions (G009, G013) each paused once, offered the opt-in tie-break, and resumed with the claimants' agreement, as the protocol requires (`docs/playtests/pilot-protocol.md:142–158`). Jun's K8 crawl at G001–G002 was rendered unnecessary by the opened hatch; no die or consequence was imposed for a moot action.

**Wrong in the command record, no effect at Stress 0 — Quick Scans were marked risky.** Each of the four Quick Scan `raw_roll` events (seeds 110001, 110004, 110007 and 110014) records `contexts:["risky"]`. Their declared failure stakes were only “no reading,” whereas the printed skin defines a risky test by a failure that materially changes the situation, such as injury, discipline, an alarm, lockdown or escalating danger (`skins/service_duct_blues.md:116–121`). The frozen P10 policy also explicitly excludes Quick Scans from risky tests (`playtests/runs/P10/policies.md`, “Risky tests”). None was charged because Stress stayed at 0. At Stress 4, this context would incorrectly invoke the skin's 1 RES or +1 Stress toll. This is a reproducibility defect in how the command was called, distinct from the correct narrated roll and from the scanner's free-use resource entry.

**Judgement call — companion help should have received an explicit pre-roll ruling.** Jun backed Imre's bedside questioning (G005–G006), Sefa was ready to support the Captain call (G013–G014), and the companions backed the corridor and ready-room exchanges (G016–G018). These Imre social tests used one die, but the Custodian never said whether the witness, corroborating evidence or support changed the odds. The Manual makes situational Advantage the Custodian's call (`rules/core/adventurers_manual.md:33–40`), so these are not proven missing bonuses. But the protocol requires the correct modifiers before commitment (`docs/playtests/pilot-protocol.md:138–149`); a one-line “your support is already in the stated stakes” or “Advantage from the witnessed record” would make each decision auditable. In contrast, holding a light at G009 and holding the access cover at G013 was compatible help that did not need a separate roll or another confirmation.

**Not assessable as a rules violation — “nobody else can pay.”** G006, G014 and G018 prohibit Sefa or Jun paying Imre's nudge. The Manual says a player may nudge a die and pay from “your own pool” (`rules/core/adventurers_manual.md:164–171`), but does not explicitly settle whether a companion may contribute chits to another character's check. The conservative ruling was stated and applied consistently; Stage 3 can decide whether this needs an explicit rule. It did not change the result here: Imre could afford each offered nudge himself.

**Correct — zero Stress on the actions shown.** The crew used no fob, did not amend a false log, did not strip a live hydroponics line, declined Brevik's corridor bargain, and finished the swap before the announced deadline. The logged, private Captain call was reasonably excluded from “in the public eye” at G013. The spare HX-40 for a signed and witnessed statement was a fair crew favour under the frozen public mapping, and no failed test met its witnessed injury/system-damage/alarm trigger. Stress therefore stayed at zero; the spotless inspection at G019 had nothing to purge. This is a successful exposure and avoidance of priced choices, not a live test of a Stress step or crisis (`playtests/runs/P10/fuse_mapping_public.md`; `skins/service_duct_blues.md:106–125`).

**Wrong — beat 6's non-perilous flag.** G012–G015 made the transducer swap the consequential choice under a running second-bell deadline. Failure meant a misreading sensor and worsening pulses; the Captain had already said an unstable loop at the bell would be vented. That could cost the immediate plant-stabilisation goal, even though Imre succeeded. The saved `p10-b6-beat-end` event says `perilous:false`. Under the revision-7 definition — failure at the beat's consequential choice could cost Stamina, a life *or the goal* — beat 6 qualifies (`docs/playtests/pilot-protocol.md:171–176`; `rules/core/custodians_almanac.md:186–201`). Beats 1 and 4 are correctly perilous on their stated injury risks. Beat 2's failed triage would have led to emergency sedation and a harder recovery, but the stated failure did not cost life or the goal; its non-perilous flag is defensible. Beats 3, 5 and 7–10 had no comparably declared goal-ending choice. Correcting beat 6 makes three perilous beats in the session rather than two. A milestone would then be *eligible* at beat 6; the Almanac and frozen policy permit awarding on the third or fourth perilous beat, so the absence of an award is not independently a proven error without a declared three-beat cadence (`rules/core/custodians_almanac.md:121–129`; `playtests/runs/P10/policies.md`, “Judging a beat perilous”). The saved history should remain frozen; future policy should state the intended cadence before play.

## Scenario and fiction

**Correct — the two private clocks follow the frozen timetable.** Cascade starts at 1, falls to 0 after the beat-1 reroute, time-ticks after undamped beats 2 and 3, is held by the K9 cutoff through beats 4 and 5, and ends with the replaced sensor in beat 6. Clean Slate advances when Brevik learns of the clip and at the ends of beats 2, 4 and 6, filling at the act break. The public replies show signs and consequences rather than clock names. The Captain's deadline appears directly at G007 in beat 3, although Brevik mentions the second bell earlier at G003; that earlier mention is forewarning, not the formal Command announcement. The archive fill leaves independent evidence usable in act 2, as frozen. The beat-7 authorised, witnessed read-only audit pull at G016 plausibly needs no roll after the Captain freezes the trail. These findings are based on the scenario file and the saved clock events, not on the orchestrator's summary (`playtests/runs/P10/frozen_hidden_scenario.md:12–47`; `playtests/runs/P10/as_closed/state/logs/session_001.jsonl`).

**Judgement call — Clean Slate's interrupt was discoverable but weakly signposted.** The crew saw Brevik's pause, Pell's tablet and the galley closure, yet before G015 the Custodian offered no concrete way to preserve the raw archive or secure a freeze, two of the frozen clock interrupts. Imre and Jun were already copying and logging other evidence, and the G015 fill was reversible through the authorised pull in G016. This is not a rules breach or a forced dead end. It did, however, make the hidden archive clock less actionable than the physical Cascade; offering a visible route to an archive copy or Dace-witnessed pull would have made the warning more useful without exposing the clock (`playtests/runs/P10/frozen_hidden_scenario.md:23–30`).

**Judgement call — NPCs and clue fidelity were strong.** Brevik's protective manner, schedule motive, tremor and eventual willingness to self-report match the frozen lines. Pell tampers and yields without laying a hand on anyone; Dace witnesses and faithfully checks the repair; Marsh trades a real spare for an honest record; Okoro grants Jun's roster place for an honest report, not a sidearm display. The Captain first demands logged evidence, then freezes the archive and acts on it. G017 option 2 says she “acts on logged evidence at once,” close to her private line and more categorical than a single observed decision supports. G016 had, however, just shown her freeze the archive on logged evidence, and the crew now held a stronger case in the same matter. The option is a locally justified promised consequence, not a demonstrated disclosure of a new hidden fact or a general right to command her future decisions.

**Wrong as fiction and relay status, but not a private-material leak — G003.** The public reply says Sefa and Jun “have not yet seen what Imre suggested.” That repeats the orchestrator's delivery-status note rather than an in-world observation. The native receipt check confirms each later received P002 Imre *before* G003 in the same delivery, so the sentence was also stale when read. It exposed no hidden scenario, private campaign state or another agent's instruction: P002 Imre's answer was a completed public contribution already queued for those players. I therefore do **not** apply the protocol's harness-only consequence for private-material leaks (`docs/playtests/pilot-protocol.md:78–86, 297–298`). This classification is narrow: the GM still let coordinator metadata contaminate the public fiction, and a similar line containing an unreleased answer or hidden state would require the leak stop rule (`playtests/runs/P10/astra_evidence_checks.json`, `checks.deliveries.items`, Sefa/Jun delivery 2).

**Wrong, low impact — G020 authors Imre's dialogue.** The stinger gives the player character the line “No” in reply to Quill without a player declaration. It fits Imre's established stance but still speaks for a seated PC. Quill could have asked the question and been left waiting, or Imre's already filed honest log could have carried the answer. This did not affect mechanics or the ending, and Imre's final player reply accepted the scene.

## Record and access audit

**Verified — the in-play relay and saved public record.** The separate native subagent traces confirm that all 20 saved GM public bodies match their checkpoint versions byte for byte and match a native public transmission. Three native public transmissions were duplicate identical reports (G004, G018 and G019), leaving 20 unique bodies. All 51 saved player deliveries match the native incoming payloads exactly after removing the fixed coordinator envelope; each contains the expected public and already-completed peer dialogue in order, including G020 to all three players. The packet files match the three players' only `Read` results and their manifest hashes; each packet contains the intended skin slices. Each player used one packet read and hand-back transport thereafter. These observations support compliance with the instructed access boundary, not enforced filesystem isolation (`playtests/runs/P10/astra_evidence_checks.json`, `checks.tool_trace`, `checks.public_checkpoints`, `checks.deliveries`, `checks.packets`).

**Wrong, post-closure only — the three final player replies were not cross-relayed.** Imre, Sefa and Jun each received G020 and answered with substantive in-character dialogue and new plans; their exact answers are saved in the transcript. None received the other two final answers, six absent peer deliveries in all. The protocol requires every completed answer to reach every player (`docs/playtests/pilot-protocol.md:261–272`), so this is a narrow transport lapse. No subsequent in-play decision depended on those lines. All pre-closure peer answers were delivered exactly once in order (`playtests/runs/P10/astra_evidence_checks.json`, `checks.deliveries.per_player`). Future closed sessions should either collect receipts without new dialogue or relay one final dialogue batch without soliciting further play declarations.

**Verified with two small boundaries.** The native Custodian trace and saved JSONL agree on 14 actual draws, seed by seed, with no retries, gaps or consumed tie-break dice. The frozen hidden scenario, policies and public mapping match their recorded hashes in initial, as-closed and final snapshots, and the 65-file as-closed and final trees are byte-identical. The Custodian did list three file names in `playtests/runs/P10/` at 06:55 while troubleshooting its own note append; this crossed the instructed path boundary but read no file content there. It also appended to its private *campaign* custodian notes after `session-close` and prompt rebuild; therefore snapshot equality does not mean literally no post-close write. The mechanics log ends at `session_end`, and the frozen sources were unchanged. Finally, the archived Custodian tool trace truncates 74 long string fields at 300 characters; the native trace, checked against its 87 call identities and timestamps, supports the access and seed findings (`playtests/runs/P10/astra_evidence_checks.json`, `checks.access_deviation`, `checks.postclose_writes`, `checks.seed_schedule`, `checks.frozen_sources`, `checks.as_closed_vs_final`).

The episode sustains both the repair and cover-up plots across a 6+4 beat structure, with two true collision pauses and no forced extra tests. The Stress=0 result shows this particular crew consistently chose the honest, procedurally safe route at stated costs. It cannot by itself establish whether the experimental mapping has a useful long-run pressure rate, because no charge, step effect, purge from above zero or crisis was exercised. The corrected perilous classification and the explicit-help question are the actionable findings for the joint review.
