## Pacing, agency and play quality

The cold opening supplied an immediate rescue, a moving patrol and a paid job with an ethical side opportunity. Success did not freeze time: the eight-minute unrolled transfer advanced the patrol clock. The twenty-minute Strain trigger was stated at G001 and made explicit again at G004; the crew departed at seventeen minutes after choosing the successful fast check. Zero Strain therefore does not itself establish a missed charge, nor validate the chosen twenty-minute threshold. This run never tested the revised additive core/skin procedure with a Pressure gain.

Risky menu options generally contained their tests and consequences before commitment. G005 paused only for Rook's meaningful post-roll nudge; the relay queued that exchange for the other players before their next decisions. There were no stalls or generic confirmation loops. The session was shorter than the protocol's usual 10–12 beats and Almanac 9's illustrative pacing. Act two contained only the jump and trade settlement, with no new setback. Delivery provided a genuine ending, but this is a brief procedural adventure rather than evidence of sustained session tension.

The crew cooperated and spoke to one another. Rook checked quarantine risks, Kest volunteered to act, and Mira protected the passengers' route information. They agreed on nearly everything, however; distinct temperament instructions did not produce a difficult conflict or a costly difference of priorities. All seven tests had Advantage from Expertise or a paid Knack. Mira's watch benefit duplicated existing Expertise at the trade test, so its grant and removal were exercised but it did not change the dice count. No causal comparison with earlier pilots is justified by these observations.

- **G003 and G005:** Kest and Rook each offered to operate the scan/cross-check. The Guildmaster combined their methods and selected Rook as the single rolling actor both times. This preserved their stated rescue objective but merits an agency judgement: did it adequately preserve Kest's intent to perform the task?
- **G003–G006:** inspect the elapsed-time account, Brant's ten-minute verification hold versus continuing approach, twenty-minute supervised window, threatened Strain, and Rook's deferred nudge. The hold was narrated as holding the interdiction order while the cutter kept approaching; the frozen scenario describes a standoff hold. It did not reach an adverse deadline, but the distinction should be assessed.
- **Beat boundaries and milestone:** rescue, diagnostics, transfer and permit were recorded as separate scenes. Transfer alone was non-perilous. Check whether the other divisions and peril labels support the milestone after beat 4. Later jump and trade were both perilous, leaving two since the award and no second milestone.
- **G006–G007:** two explicit jump hazards and two tests, with no Engineer quota test; Scout Surveyor payment, clean-jump Fuel +1, and watch benefit. The watch's relay hazard was introduced at the act opening rather than fixed in the scenario. Check the fiction supporting it and the Expertise application.
- **G007–G008:** concrete cargo acquisition, conditional job acceptance, a single net trade reward, one-use stake consumption, nonstacking Advantage and removal of the watch marker. Merchant Broker was publicly available but not used because the trade succeeded; no reroll path was tested.
- **Closure continuity:** the append-only recap retains old pending offer, quarantine and watch-benefit entries alongside later resolved summaries. Its final NPC/thread entries still call Ilya's introduction owed, whereas G008 says she sent it. A future bootstrap must reconcile this; the record was not repaired after play.

No invented rule or engine failure was identified by the orchestrator as a confirmed finding. The audit should distinguish wrong rulings from judgement calls and untested branches. All 95 commands in the recorder succeeded, including setup and help calls; the seven dice commands used consecutive allocated seeds.
