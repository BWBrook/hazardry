# P3 independent audit

P3 completed a solo Mournful Shores session under protocol revision 4 (`849a8e0`). G030 disclosed the entire crisis table that the protocol withholds from players throughout the dialogue, so the protocol's Leaks rule (`docs/playtests/pilot-protocol.md:224–225`) classifies **the whole run as a harness test only**. Its recorded play still provides useful procedure and pacing evidence: a time-driven threat, player-chosen Insanity costs, a correctly recorded Break, and a successful Unspeakable rite. It cannot count toward the programme's uncontaminated role-boundary or game-play evidence. The remaining concerns are adjudication and presentation choices, not demonstrated engine failures.

**Verdicts:** Correct: the logged Break, Unspeakable cost and resolution, milestone, advancement and closure below. Wrong: G030's protocol leak, the omitted public session log, and the two chronology statements. Judgement call: the timing of first witnessing, the G029 puzzle hint, beat boundaries and perilous flags. Not assessable from retained files: the 37 overwritten historical checkpoints, whether an unrolled backlash would have been applied correctly, or whether revision 4 guidance itself caused the observed behavior.

## Findings that affect interpretation

1. **Crisis table disclosed to the player (confirmed protocol breach).** The packet excludes the Mournful Shores d6 crisis table, as required by `docs/playtests/pilot-protocol.md:62–76`, and the protocol says players *never receive* crisis tables. The packet hash matches the manifest, and a search of `packet_nell.md` finds no table entries. G030 nevertheless prints all six outcomes before Nell chooses the Break (`transcript.md:649–668`, `replies/gm_030.md`). This is a dialogue-time leak of withheld material, not merely a mistake in the initial packet. Protocol `:224–225` therefore limits the entire run to harness-test status. This is a pilot information boundary, not a claim that the published game's table is secret.

2. **Witnessing Insanity was charged late (confirmed timing, subjective threshold).** G001 shows two breathless drowned men in Nell's view (`transcript.md:5–9`). The frozen scenario and policies require +1 for the first sight of a new class of horror, at most once per scene (`frozen_hidden_scenario.md:56–59`, `policies.md`). The sole ambient charge occurs at G014, after several interactions, when Silas's face comes within a foot of the lantern (`transcript.md:266–272`; log event `p3-b1-witness-thrall`, sequence 53). A Keeper could judge that close view to be the first *full* confrontation; the written policy says first sight, and G001 already supplies it. The late charge left Nell no chance to choose a route before that cost. Score this as a rules-adjudication weakness, not a false log entry or a second charge due.

3. **A private puzzle solution became an offered action (confirmed overhint, not a mechanical breach).** The frozen scenario says Maud can be turned for a verse only by Ansel's childhood name for her (`frozen_hidden_scenario.md:45–49,69`). G029 offers “say Maud's name to her as he did when he was small” as option 2 (`transcript.md:622–643`). The surrounding public clues made Ansel's role and a backwards verse plausible, but this specific childhood-name lever had not been established by a player discovery. It narrows the investigation by giving the answer in a menu. Nell chose another route, so the overhint did not itself determine the outcome.

4. **Two continuity slips are real but local.** G025 says Gant kept the matter quiet for “thirty years,” and G036 describes eleven years of Harrow payments, while the frozen chronology puts the drownings in 1921 and play in 1924 (`frozen_hidden_scenario.md:5–7`; `transcript.md` G025 and `:796–806`). They do not alter the logged tests, but the ledger error changes what the supposedly incriminating document proves. Correct those statements in any narrative reuse; retain the original transcript as run evidence.

5. **The campaign has no public session log (confirmed records omission).** The final campaign includes its mechanical JSONL log and memory recap, but no public Markdown session log under `state/logs/`; the Custodian's final handback explicitly reports that `validate_campaign.py` warned “no public session log yet” and that it did not run `tools/session_log.py` (`tool_trace.jsonl`, final Custodian handback). Repository `AGENTS.md:27` requires the fictional consequences in both memory and the public log. The pilot's saved 38-reply transcript preserves the fiction for this audit, so this omission does not invalidate the mechanical session closure; it does leave the campaign workspace short of its normal resumable record.

## Revision 4 and play quality

The cold open immediately put Ansel at risk, and act 2 opened on the Station flood. The Tide Turns advanced on a timetable and opposition moves without player failures; the Station flood clock then advanced on its own schedule. This behavior matches revision 4's intended threat procedure. The pilot changed the skin, party size, scenario and player as well as the guidance, so it cannot isolate the guidance's effect. Most Pressure came from declared rite, tome and toll choices; only one point was ambient, and that point was late. Thus P3 shows that this bold solo player willingly bought Insanity; it does not establish that the Keeper reliably charges environmental horror at first exposure. The player also stopped her ears to avoid the choir trigger; the Custodian honoured that choice and its hearing cost.

The solo player made consequential decisions consistent with her drive and line: she repeatedly risked resources for Ansel, bargained instead of forcing conflict, chose the Break with explicit danger, then returned for documentary proof. The session reached a coherent ending in six unusually dense beats; 24 of 38 GM replies belonged to beat 1. This is a pacing observation, not a failure to reach the Almanac's illustrative ten-beat session. The two combats, parley and changing question in the cistern could arguably have supported a beat boundary earlier, but the persistent location and continuing question of Ansel's rescue make the recorded single beat defensible (`rules/core/custodians_almanac.md:186–195`).

The report's harness gap is real at the interface used: two opposed lantern jabs inflicted fictional damage on Silas, but the engine retained 4/4 Stamina because these were `opposed` events, while `keeper_notes.md:2–3` carried the effective 3 then 2. This did not decide the fight: Silas never reached 0 and the Unspeakable ending removed the thralls. It is a state-fidelity limitation for future combat tests, not proof that this P3 outcome was misresolved. The skin names backlash for failed Incantations/Unspeakable rites without printing a specific table; the Keeper declared one before play, but no backlash was rolled, so this run does not validate that policy.

No current code fix follows from the P3 outcome. Possible next-cycle changes, **not adopted by this audit**, are to clarify the public treatment of crisis-table risk, charge or telegraph first witnessing at the declared trigger, avoid giving a hidden solution as an option, restore the missing public session log, and consider a tracked way to apply NPC harm awarded by an opposed test. The two continuity lines can be corrected in a derivative narrative without rewriting the frozen transcript.
