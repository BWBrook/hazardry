# P9 report (orchestrator)

P9 was Briar & Benedictine with two investigators, at the grim budget, under protocol revision 6. It is the first run of the second tranche, and Barry asked that it be a mystery.

The Custodian built a sound case. Every conclusion had three leads, every clue reached the players, and the case resolved on evidence.
- Two investigators who are strong in Mercy and weak in Lore solved it almost entirely by interrogation and testimony, which shows the skin's mystery procedure working.
- **Sin never moved.** Every Sin-bearing danger the scenario offered had an honest way round, and the players took it every time.
- **Revision 6's agency rules held.** No roller was chosen silently, every costly spend was offered at its price, and no failed test was retried. Holding to them cost round trips, because the two players answered at the same time and kept claiming the same roll.

The session ran 12 beats over two acts (7 + 5), with 27 Custodian replies. All 27 checkpoints matched, each kept as its own version. These are the orchestrator's notes; Astra's audit decides the rules questions.

## What happened

**Act 1, seven beats (G001–G022). Day one, 12 December 1142.**
- **The mill-leat** (beat 1, perilous). In the cold open, a boy is on breaking ice over a body. Anselm reaches him (FLT with Advantage), and the body is Edric, an old bowman. Anselm hears Wat out; Hawise reads Wulfric's face. Insight confirms that Wulfric knew it was Edric. Hawise pays 3 beads to read the wound clearly: one mallet blow from behind, dead before the ice, rope galls on both wrists.
- **The almonry barn** (beat 2). Osmer offers a bargain for quiet (+1 Sin, stated plainly), and both refuse. Hawise wins against Osmer, who names Pell and lets slip "at the works". Maud's test fails.
- **The guest-house** (beat 3). Hawise loses to Pell, declining the 3-bead nudge, and Pell warns Osmer (Winter Tally 1 → 2). His emptied room yields the burnt vellum.
- **The poor-pit and the Abbot** (beat 4). The sexton gives two soft skulls and Wulfric's hand in the burial book. The Abbot's door needs four pauses to settle who speaks, decided at last by an agreed tie-break die. The Abbot gives a licensed dig, a licence to ask in his name, and the Rule of three. Hawise beats Wulfric publicly (Tally +1), and three witnesses get the postern barred (Tally −2). Wulfric is ordered held.
- **The dig** (beat 5). Hawise faces down the Prior. Both skulls are dented the same way, and Wulfric has fled. The Prior's bargain for silence (+1 Sin) is refused. The gate-porter testifies.
- **Dusk** (beats 6–7, act end). Both questionings fail on natural 19s, one of them drawn from a reused seed. Compline (Tally +1).

**Act 2, five beats (G022–G027). Day two, before dawn.**
- **The barn at Lauds** (beat 8, perilous). Osmer's work party is turned back: Anselm rolls a natural 1 with the men, and Hawise beats Osmer. Godwin names Gilbert.
- **The infirmary loft and the mill** (beats 9–10). Gilbert is alive and tells the central truth: the castle undercroft, Wulfric's mallet, the cellarer's order. Hawise rolls a natural 20 with Alys; the frost prints still deliver the clue.
- **The carry** (beat 11). Gilbert is carried unseen with Advantage from Hawise's watch.
- **Prime in the Abbot's chamber** (beat 12, perilous, act end). The Prior demands Anselm's informants, aiming straight at Anselm's temperament line, and Anselm refuses. Gilbert testifies, and Hawise names Osmer on four witnesses. The undercroft chest yields the roll ("candles to the Lord Prior, 9s a week"). Osmer is held, and the Prior is confined pending the Bishop.
- **Close.** The milestone came at the third perilous beat.

**Reading.** P9 repeats P8 more strongly, and fits the scenario-framing finding of round 4.
- The Custodian put Sin on the table at every turning point, priced it and announced it. The players, as their temperaments allowed, always found the honest route instead: refusing both bribes, licensing the dig, standing on the Abbot's licence rather than defying the Prior.
- The skin's own Sin triggers (lying under oath, breaking a vow of peace, disturbing a sanctified grave, petty Insight) are moral lapses. Characters played to an honest temperament simply never commit them.
- **Briar's Pressure is opt-in vice.** For an honest AI table it is effectively off unless the scenario makes the honest route impossible or more costly than sin.

That is a design observation for Barry, not a Custodian failure. A human table might well sin. These players were given lines they keep.

**Luck.**
- Anselm spent 2 beads, both on Insight; Hawise spent 7 (3 on Insight, 4 on nudges).
- None was spent to avoid Pressure, because none was ever at stake on a roll.
- Both recovered at the Compline rest, and refilled at the milestone.

## Stalls, invented content and rulings to audit

- **No stalls.**
- **Actor collisions.**
  - *Pattern:* six pure pauses, most caused by simultaneous answers. The players claimed the same roll, then each deferred to the other, then each claimed it again.
  - *The fix in play:* the Custodian's opt-in tie-break die (G013, seed 109009) ended the deadlock without the Custodian choosing.
  - *Learning:* by mid-act 2 the players settled tie-breaks in advance in their own answers (G019 onwards), with no further collisions over a roll.
- **For the audit:**
  1. **Seed reuse (G022).** The Dunstan roll used 109013, already used for Lambert; both came up natural 19. The Custodian reported it and settled as drawn. The schedule resumed at 109015.
  2. **Scenario change after the freeze (G009).** Pell leaves "after None today", not on the next morning. The hidden file was not updated to match.
  3. **The scenario file edited at close.** The run-log line of the campaign's copy was changed after the last prompt rebuild, making the prompt stale. The frozen copy in `runs/` is canonical.
  4. **Clock names in public.** "The Winter Tally clock" (G016 onwards) and "Chapter Summons" appear in public text. No values were disclosed.
  5. **A failure-branch clue disclosed before the spend decision** (G008 and G010). Both players then declined the spend. This could make paying to succeed look less worthwhile.
  6. **A dominated option (G019).** Contesting the Prior risked a tick for no gain over carrying on digging under the licence at no cost.
  7. **Discovery-tick accounting (G017).** The Custodian's private note treats this tick as "the second and last of at most two". The frozen scenario counts Pell's warning separately from the cap of two discovery ticks.
  8. **Paying for a companion's roll.** The Custodian ruled twice (G004, G014) that one player's beads cannot pay for the other's roll. The Manual is silent on this, so it is a Stage 3 question.
  9. **The natural-20 complication** at G024 was not invoked. That is a judgement call; the Manual says a natural 20 "usually" brings one.
  10. **Over-length replies.** 10 of 27 were over 400 words (mean 345, longest 440).
  11. **Routing (G025).** The text invited Hawise to change anything, but the reply was addressed only to Anselm.
  12. **"The Prior's chaplain"** at G018 should have been the Abbot's chaplain; the Custodian reported it.

## Harness gaps

- **No defects.** Opposed tests, NPC registration, Insight resources, `--defer` and settle, the rest recovery, the tie-break draw through `roll.py table`, the milestone, and the session close all worked.
- **The primary bias in `gen_character.py`** is a weight, and at 0 build points it gave a LOR-biased character LOR 8. Not a defect, but worth knowing when a run needs a particular competence.

## Rules-text gaps

- **Briar's Sin triggers are all vices.** The Sin track moves only when a character sins or witnesses grave evil. A design question for Barry: should Briar have an ambient trigger, such as time under suspicion, the abbey's siege, or proximity to evil? Under Barry's additive decision, Almanac 4's time-under-threat could apply. The Custodian did not use it, and the scenario's declared ambient triggers were all tied to vices.
- **Paying for an ally's roll.** One sentence in Manual 3 would settle whether Luck can fund a companion's roll.
- **The Sin track and crisis table sit in the Custodian section,** though players need them.

## Coherence and fun

A strong mystery and the most procedurally faithful run of the pilot.
- **Every lead converged.** The case resolved on testimony and evidence under the skin's own Rule of three, which the Custodian turned into the Abbot's condition.
- **The temperaments shaped the outcome.** Anselm refused to name Osmer without three sides, and the Abbot could not act on the cellarer until Ivo's testimony. He then refused the Prior his informants, and the Abbot upheld him.
- **The NPCs kept to their declared lines.** The Prior never raised his voice, and Osmer never struck anyone.

What it lacked was risk: no Sin, no Stamina lost, and two perilous beats out of eleven before the last.

## What to change before the programme

1. **Players: settle collisions in the declaration.** Add a player-brief line such as: "If your companion may also want a roll you want, say in the same answer who should take it, or accept a tie-break." The players learned this themselves in act 2.
2. **Custodian: offer the tie-break.** When two players claim one roll, offer an opt-in tie-break die at once. Keep asking, never choosing.
3. **Scenarios for honest temperaments.** For skins whose Pressure is vice-only, include at least one danger whose honest route is also costly. Revision 6 C allows this; it should not be a quota.
4. **Stage 3:**
   - Briar's ambient Sin triggers (for Barry);
   - Luck paying for a companion's roll;
   - where the Pressure track and crisis table sit in the book.

## Post-audit note

Astra's audit (`audit.md`) is accepted. It corrects this report as follows:
- **P9 counts as a harness test only.**
  - G016 published Wulfric's private boundary as a guarantee: he "will not strike a child or fight in the church". Private clock names and ticks also appeared in public, so the protocol's Leaks clause applies.
  - The disclosure was limited and came through narration. The classification is strict, but correct under the clause as written.
- **The Chapter Summons clock missed a definite tick at G006.** Frozen line 57 charges it for openly questioning the cellarer, whether or not the questioner succeeds. The clock ending at 0 is wrong.
- **Insight was mishandled several times:**
  - G003 and G018 omitted Hawise's eligible offers.
  - G008 wrongly told Anselm his barn-scene Insight was spent, which withheld an affordable nudge-plus-Insight option.
  - G023 closed beat 8 before the offered Insights were decided.
  - "Every meaningful affordable option was offered" is therefore wrong.
- **Two relay omissions were mine.** Anselm's P007 and P024 answers never reached Hawise verbatim. The manifest's claim that all queued answers were delivered needs these exceptions.
- **Other corrections:**
  - The threatened Chapter tick at G019 contradicted the active licence.
  - Beats 6 and 7 were different places, not a prayer-only split.
  - Beat 4, by contrast, ran several location cuts together.
- **"Briar's Pressure is opt-in vice" overstates the case.** Core time under threat stays additive, failed healing and the skin's fear portent need no vice, and the scenario's non-urgent-only ambient policy and quiet Chapter clock restricted exposure. P9 does not show that honest play switches off the skin.
