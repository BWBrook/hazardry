## Observed under revision 7

**Actor collisions were resolved promptly in P10 and P13.**
- **P10.** Two pure pauses, each settled in one reply.
- **P13.** The one-rope conflict (G008) was settled inside a reply that also restated both methods' stakes.
- **In every case:**
  - the Custodian asked the claimants to choose and offered the opt-in tie-break;
  - the players converged at once and declined the die;
  - reserved tie-break seeds passed cleanly to the next real roll.
- **The new player-brief line was used from the first turn.** In P10, three players named the same speaker unprompted.
- **Compare P9:** six pauses, four of them in one chain.

**Ability offers improved.**
- P10 offered every optional power at its cost before rolls, announced when once-per-scene uses refreshed, and applied both natural-1 extras.
- **Two gaps remain:**
  - **Help.** The Custodian never stated whether a companion's support gave Advantage (P10 G005, G014, G017).
  - **Wickglow.** P13 never mentioned it, the one capability that answered the run's main pressure (light), when the lantern died.

**Private material was kept private.**
- Neither run named a private clock, and NPC limits appeared as speech or action in the moment.
- **P10 G017** stated the Captain's line as an option's consequence. The audit judged it over-categorical but justified by G016; it is not a leak.
- **P10 G003** narrated my relay-status note: stale metadata, not a leak.

**Scenario fidelity was good, with one disputed ruling (P13 G017).**
- Timetables and frozen damps were followed, seeds were continuous, and the frozen sources were unchanged.
- **P13 G017.** Fable reads it as ruling out, in public, the frozen room-5 solution ("re-seated weight"). Astra reads it as over-categorical and a possible narrowing only. The disagreement is preserved in `P13/audit.md`.
- **P13's frozen scenario contradicted itself** about the bell-well lip (lines 17 and 33).
- **P13's brace delay** was applied literally to "the next scheduled tick", so two ticks fell in successive turns. The frozen text did not say whether a delay shifts the whole schedule.

**Perilous flags erred in both directions.**
- **P10 too strict.** Beat 6, a timed repair on which the goal depended, should have been perilous.
- **P13 too generous.** One weight-house scene was split into two, and the second part flagged perilous after G005 had declared the work safe. The milestone came an act early.
- Revision 7's wording was right both times. What failed was applying it, so examples will help more than more words.

**Records.**
- **P10's relay script.** All 51 deliveries were composed from the record and verified against what the players received, which closes P9's omission class. The closing lines were not cross-relayed.
- **P13's closing.** Receipt-only replies plus one archive batch is the better method.
- **P13's transcript** was written by two processes at once and doubled from G009. Astra found the cause and fixed it.
- **Command contexts.** P10 logged four Quick Scans with the `risky` context. That was harmless at Stress 0, but wrong at step 4.
- **Agency.** P10 G020 wrote a line of dialogue for a player character.
- **Custodian isolation.** P13's Custodian read an aggregate memory store that holds earlier pilot findings. This is not a player leak, but it limits claims about the Custodian's independence.

## Pressure and the experimental mapping

The mapping did what it was designed to do:
- **Generic danger never fed a distinctive fuse.** In P10 that meant steam, flash-burns and an airway. In P13 it meant darkness, the release clock and inspection.
- **Players reasoned with it.** In P10, Jun named an off-book deal as such, and Imre checked whether a private call counted as public before he made it.

**Both runs ended at Pressure 0.**
- **P10.** The crew refused or averted every priced temptation: the fob, the log amendment, the corridor bribe, stripping a line, and the deadline.
- **P13.** The single mapped exposure (the current) was avoided by a dry-ground cast.

Across the second tranche, Pressure has moved only where the fiction made exposure hard to avoid: P12's time under threat. These crews consistently selected the offered honest or careful routes at costs they accepted. The comparison is not controlled. Zero Pressure shows the mappings applied and avoided, not that generic danger cannot feed a distinctive fuse; P13's physical exposure explicitly could.

As Astra says, this does not justify a quota. Two narrow things follow:
- a mapping should say, at setup, where its exposures are **unavoidable in the fiction**, if any;
- a **labelled, frozen probe** with fiction-warranted unavoidable exposure can check trigger application, charge accounting and step and crisis consequences. It cannot estimate a normal-play rate or isolate the mapping's effect. A rate comparison, if it ever matters, needs matched opportunities and conditions, not a quota in ordinary runs. The probe is not retrofitted into P11.

Whether human players behave the same is outside the pilot's reach.

## Proposals: protocol revision 8, for P11

**A. Scenes and peril by example.** Add three worked lines to the Custodian brief's perilous-beat item:
- "A timed repair or attempt whose failure would materially threaten the goal is perilous, even if it succeeds" (P10 beat 6).
- "Continuous work with the same place, purpose and conflict is one scene however many exploration turns it takes; safe routine completion is not another perilous scene" (P13 beats 2–3; Astra's wording).
- "A real trap defeated by inspection or preparation is perilous" (P13 beat 7).

**B. Help.** Add one clause to the correct-numbers line: "When a companion helps, say before the roll whether the help gives Advantage, and why."

**C. Contexts.** Add: "Pass the `risky` context only when the stated failure would materially change the situation." This clarifies the brief and the handbook; the skin text already says it.

**D. Hazards and frozen solutions.** Add: "Describe hazards by what the characters observe. Distinguish observed danger from established impossibility, and never rule out in public an approach your private notes allow in the current fictional state" (P13 G017). This mirrors the existing "never offer an option your notes rule out". A recorded flood, collapse or player action can still legitimately close a route; the initial notes are not a promise that every solution stays open. The G017 disagreement does not need resolving to adopt the principle.

**E. Player characters.** Add to the handbook: "Never write a player character's words or decisions; leave them to the player" (P10 G020).

**F. Scenario setup:**
- "Say whether each clock delay or interrupt shifts the whole schedule or only the next tick."
- The orchestrator checks the frozen scenario for internal contradictions before freezing it (P13 lines 17 and 33).

**G. Records and isolation:**
- The orchestrator alone writes the transcript. The Custodian never writes relay records (P13).
- **Closing.** Collect any final in-character contributions, relay all completed peer closing lines once, and require literal receipt-only acknowledgements to that archive, with no further game turn. Skip the archive if every reply was already a pure receipt (P13's method, refined).
- Relay notes to the Custodian carry delivery state only when needed, and the Custodian does not narrate them (P10 G003).
- **Custodian access boundary, made explicit.** Allowed: its own campaign's state and memory, the frozen scenario, the canonical rules, and its assigned run evidence. Excluded: other pilots' records, audits and reviews, other roles' histories, and any external or personal memory store (P13).

**H. Resources.** Extend the existing distinctive-capability line in the handbook: "When a resource runs out, name any capability that could answer it, with its test, cost and scope, as an option, never a requirement" (P13 Wickglow).

**I. Fuse mappings.** P11 is the base game with no skin, so no experimental mapping applies; the additive core default governs. Mappings move to the probes (below).
