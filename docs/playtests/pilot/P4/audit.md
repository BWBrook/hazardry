# P4 independent audit

P4 completed a coherent Twilight of the Northlands session on rules/harness commit `7cc705c`, under pilot protocol revision 5. The recorded arithmetic for the eventual combat settlements, Reckoning, Invocation costs, purge and crisis reset reconciles. The principal failures were in preserving player decisions: wrong damage advice, withheld affordable nudges, an extra entreaty roll, and unilateral resolution of competing declarations. No private-material leak was found in the retained public material or player tool trace. P4 remains useful play/harness evidence with these qualifications; it is not a clean adjudication run or balance evidence.

This audit leaves the frozen campaign and transcript unchanged. Rule references below are to the pinned source, verified unchanged in the working tree. `GNNN` identifies a Custodian public reply; `pNNN_name` its following player answer. Event numbers refer to `playtests/runs/P4/final/p4_twilight/state/logs/session_001.jsonl`. Three separate read-only checks covered combat arithmetic, evidence integrity and the act/Pressure state contracts; the auditor integrated their findings with a review of all public replies, player declarations relevant to the findings, the complete event sequence, scenario and policies.

## Confirmed adjudication and presentation errors

1. **Wrong — damage advice changed Hild's available choice (G008–G009, events 37–40).** Hild's STR 15 roll of 8 gives attacker margin 7; the watchman's STR 11 roll of 8 gives defence margin 3. Effective margin 4 determines the gritty Injury threshold, not ordinary damage. The correct damage is `1 + 2 + floor(7/5) - 1 = 3`. G008 says 2, offers a one-token nudge to deal 3, and says killing the 4-Stamina enemy costs six tokens. In fact no nudge deals 3 and lowering Hild's die to 5 costs three tokens and deals 4. Hild accepted the misdescribed baseline. G009 acknowledged the error and the engine applied 3, but that cannot restore the lost informed choice. We cannot assume she would have bought the kill. Rules: Adventurer's Manual 3 and 6.2 (`rules/core/adventurers_manual.md:166–171,268`); Twilight Injury uses effective margin separately (`skins/twilight_of_the_northlands.md:219–231`).

2. **Wrong — affordable, consequential nudges were withheld (G009 and G013).** G009 settles Corwen's shot, roll 19 against NIM 15 versus defence margin 5, because ten tokens are deemed “not a meaningful choice.” Corwen had 11 Hope. Lowering his own die to 9 for ten Hope gives margin 6, wins and kills the already wounded watchman; contrary to the narration, both dice need not move. G013 similarly settles Merewen's entreaty, roll 18 against HRT 11, because seven tokens are deemed too expensive. Event 76 records ten Hope still available. Seven Hope would turn that failed attempt into success. Costliness is the player's decision. Neither case meets the brief's exception for no meaningful legal post-roll choice. Rules: Manual 3 and 5.1 (`:166–171,204–211`); protocol `docs/playtests/pilot-protocol.md:122–127`; handbook `skills/agent_dm_handbook.md:50–55`.

   This is not a criticism of every immediate settlement. G012's Merewen attack needed eleven points of adjustment to beat defence margin 3; she had nine Hope and had already used her scene's Companionship nudge. That failure could properly settle without a choice. Successful attacks that already felled their targets likewise had no useful damage purchase.

3. **Wrong — a second spokesperson retried the same entreaty (G012–G013, events 75–78).** The offered action said one spokesperson. Merewen and Corwen both nominated themselves; the Custodian first used all three companions' protection offers as Merewen's Advantage, then gave Corwen another roll after her failure on the ground that his Warden pledge was different. That pledge was already part of the first roll's leverage, and both tests have the same stated objective and failure consequence. There was no new opportunity or separately accepted cost. Resolve who speaks before the one roll; do not replace a failed company entreaty with another character's roll. Rules: Twilight assemblies (`skins/twilight_of_the_northlands.md:184–192`), Manual `:211`, protocol `:129–133`. This error bypassed Gerrin's refusal and materially advanced the investigation.

4. **Wrong — majority selection overrode competing declarations (G016 and G018).** At `p015_merewen` and `p017_merewen`, Merewen explicitly says she guides; Hild nominates Corwen, who nominates himself. The Custodian says two named Corwen and selects him, retaining Merewen's contribution only as description. No majority-vote procedure was agreed. At the crossing, Hild and Corwen also each declared they would go first; G018 sends Corwen first and Hild last without resolving that conflict. Choosing one guide and avoiding a quota of extra tests is correct, but the brief requires a decision for truly incompatible declarations and forbids overriding one to make another succeed. Rule: protocol `:129–133`. The appropriate guide or crossing order is a judgement; silently supplying the group's choice is the procedural error.

5. **Wrong — the withdrawn bank interrogation still advanced Gerrin's Nerve (G004–G005, event 20).** The private turn-4 note explicitly says the clock has not ticked and will do so when the question is actually pressed. G004 seeks commitment; both players withdraw in `p004_hild` and `p004_merewen`. Nevertheless event 20 adds the pressing tick. The preceding declarations contained spoken questions, but the Custodian had explicitly left this action awaiting commitment. Applying its hidden consequence after withdrawal contradicts that handling. The two catch-up ticks for Watch sign 2 and the lantern's presence have separate scenario grounds, though they were recorded late. The extra tick made Nerve reach 4 rather than 3 at event 68. Gerrin did not ultimately flee, because the company watched him and secured the confession, so do not invent a downstream loss. Sources: `custodian_notes.md:19,23`; frozen scenario `:103–112`; protocol `:114–119`; player brief `:150–152`.

6. **Wrong — rest benefits were underpaid (G013–G014, event 81).** The “1 Hope or 1 Stamina” wording occurs at **G013**, not G012 as the orchestrator report says. A short rest gives both benefits, capped independently; all Hope pools happened to be full here, so that wording caused no observed Hope loss. Merewen then explicitly requested good care with Wenna's poultice, and G014 accepted the poultice but restored only one Stamina (2→3). Good care gives two instead, so that accepted care should have restored her to 4. The later one-point camp recovery would then reach 5 rather than the logged final 4. No subsequent damage occurred. Rules: Manual `:180,191–194`. If Wenna's aid were intended not to qualify, the Custodian needed to say so rather than accept the good-care description and silently use the lesser benefit.

7. **Wrong — some public rules information was missing or misleading.** G002 and G003 omit the actual dice and margins for the rescue and parley, although events 5, 8 and 14 preserve them. The settlements succeed correctly, but Almanac 8 explicitly requires showing the result and margin (`rules/core/custodians_almanac.md:180`). G005 says the look-out dice 20 and 18 can both move because neither is a natural 1; natural 20 is also locked (Manual `:167`). The kept ordinary 18 was legally nudgeable, and Corwen declined, so no illegal nudge occurred. G020's combat option presents Merewen's Break as permitting speech or defence only. Her actual Break rule removes nudging and assigns a portrayed impulse; the defend-or-speak restriction belongs to the separate **Black despair** crisis result (Twilight `:110,158`). No attempted action was rejected on that basis, but the menu misstated her options.

A gritty trigger was initially presented, but no Deflection test was ultimately resolved.
- **Correct — G010–G012:** Merewen's round-one spear hit kills watchman 4. In round two, Corwen wins his defence; Hild's two-point nudge is funded by one Companionship plus one Hope, taking attacker margin 3→5 and damage 2→3. Merewen cannot afford to turn her later miss, and Corwen's final shot kills watchman 3. The two damage-advice/choice errors at G008–G009 are detailed above; there is no resulting engine arithmetic error.
- Combat ends with both watchmen at 0 Stamina, Merewen at 2, Hild's Hope 9 and Companionship 1 before milestone refill. Defeated watchman 4 remains in the round-two position map, but never acts or affects the outcome; it is stale inert data, not a demonstrated extra turn. No character becomes Injured, so Injury penalties and Vanguard exclusion were not exercised.

## Judgement calls and scenario consistency

- **G002 Wardens' word: unresolved false-option concern.** The public menu promises that a successful WIS/HRT attempt is accepted. The same turn's private note says Corwen's modern countersign “will fail,” while the true one is undiscovered (`custodian_notes.md:10`). Offering a present lead to investigate is allowed; offering a roll whose advertised success the hidden ruling forbids would be wrong. No player chose it, so the actual settlement is **not assessable**. Do not label this a demonstrated private-information leak: the hidden answer was not disclosed.
- **G017 clean rise and revised Scout failure:** the earlier fiction puts the rise behind the tainted water, and players explicitly ask to camp there. Honouring that geography is reasonable; the new check keeps a random-hazard failure but drops automatic tainted camping. It favours the players and was introduced before rolling, not after an observed failure. This is a change of proposed method and location, not an established outcome-driven reroll.
- **G018 no extra rope roll:** the Guide found a safe line with a natural 1 and the party had a rope. Resolving the careful crossing without an additional uncertain danger is defensible (Manual `:56–58`). A natural 1 does not universally waive other hazards; here no separate rope stakes had been committed. This ruling does not excuse the conflicting crossing-order declarations.
- **G015 act-two opener:** the company is already navigating a vanishing fen route with an immediate lost-route/Fatigue stake. It is quieter than the rescue but broadly fits an in-motion opening. The protocol explicitly says no forced fight is necessary (`:91–94`); “a navigation test, not a crisis” alone is not a breach. Leaving the wagon-bound NPCs in sanctuary follows the agreed journey and geography, though a short confirmation could preserve greater player control.
- **Clock and scene boundaries:** the Watch advances on successful night scenes, pauses in sanctuary/fire and falls at dawn. Its frozen “successful parley/vigil oath” hold (`frozen_hidden_scenario.md:90`) is broader than the G003 parley's granted withdrawal while the Watch follows; treat that as ambiguous scenario wording, not proof all later ticks were forbidden. Gerrin's withdrawn-action tick is the firmer error above. The first three perilous scenes—rescue, dangerous parley and combat—support the recorded milestone. Guide-only failure costs were Fatigue, so the travel scenes' non-perilous flags are defensible. Camp hazard severity and combining the two rites plus overnight vigil into beat 9 deserve judgement: the frozen rites each “take a scene,” while the record treats the climax as one continuing problem. The logged nine beats are accurate; do not present them as the uniquely correct segmentation.
- **Rite order and vigil terms:** Calling before Seating follows the need for an awake Watch and was stated before commitment. Letting one volunteer's vigil protect their companions fits the Captain's promise but is not a printed universal oath rule. No player was bound without their declaration; all three actually swore.

## Consequences for the pilot review

P4 shows that a three-player table can reach a declared shared crisis through the harness while retaining individual role histories. It also shows that correct engine arithmetic cannot protect agency when the Custodian gives bad numbers, rejects an expensive legal spend, or supplies another roll after failure. These are the priority corrections for the next brief or practice review; no source rule change is adopted by this audit.

The run supports narrower claims than “all tiers and penalties validated”: every magic tier was rolled, the step-3 toll was paid, and the crisis/backlash were generated and recorded. No Deflection settlement, actual Injury, step-2 dread test, step-4 test, crisis-penalised social test, or backlash-restricted stealth test occurred. Nor can the better menu pacing be attributed to revision 5 alone: scenario, skin, party and draws differ from earlier runs. Frozen records should retain the errors, with any later report corrections made explicitly in derivative review documents.
